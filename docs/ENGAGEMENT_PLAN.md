# Engagement Plan — Production & Merchandising Management

How to onboard, operate, and add value inside the organisation as the Senior Production & Merchandising Manager.

## 1. Context & Mandate

The organisation is a **luxury ethnic wear / bridal couture** house. Garments are made-to-order and for retail partners, spanning sampling, fabric sourcing, trims, two embroidery hubs (Kolkata, Mumbai), master cutters, stitchers, finishing, QC, packing and dispatch.

Division of responsibility:

- **Production & Merchandising Manager (you):** complete workflow control — calendar, POs, order sheets, job cards, trackers, capacity, deadlines, escalations, WIP reporting, costing coordination.
- **Master:** pattern cutting, fitting, garment construction.
- **Supporting teams:** tailors, karigars, embroidery units, vendors, sourcing, accounts, retail partners.

## 2. First 90 Days — Operating Rhythm

### Days 0–15: Baseline & Map
- Map every live order: PO number, retailer, styles, quantities, promised delivery dates.
- Walk the floor: capture the Master's cutting queue, both embroidery hubs' capacity, finishing/QC team size.
- Build the master production calendar (all deliveries, forward 12 weeks).
- Audit current trackers (Excel) and identify single source of truth.

### Days 15–45: Standardise & Digitise
- Deploy the Production & Merchandising app (this repo) as the single source of truth.
- Issue style-wise order sheets and job cards for every style in production.
- Stand up the daily WIP tracker; require daily updates from each hub.
- Agree escalation thresholds: material shortage, embroidery delay, capacity overload, QC rework.

### Days 45–90: Optimise & Prove
- Weekly production meeting with Creative Director, Master, sourcing, accounts.
- Daily WIP report to leadership; weekly delivery-risk summary.
- Lock reorder/approval workflow with retailers (sampling approval gate).
- Measure: on-time delivery %, rework %, capacity utilisation.

## 3. Weekly Operating Model

| Day | Activity |
|---|---|
| Monday | Refresh master calendar; issue weekly plan; expedite weekend promises |
| Tuesday | Embroidery hub (Kolkata/Mumbai) status call; fabric & trim reorders |
| Wednesday | Mid-week WIP review; Master fitting queue; style approvals to retailers |
| Thursday | Capacity check; vendor/costing sync with accounts; QC rework follow-up |
| Friday | Weekly WIP report, delivery-risk list, next-week capacity plan |

## 4. Escalation & Risk Matrix

| Signal | Action | Escalate to |
|---|---|---|
| Fabric/trim shortage > 3 days | Immediate reorder or substitute with approval | Sourcing, Creative Director |
| Embroidery slip > 5 days | Re-prioritise hub queue, split load | Embroidery units |
| QC failure rate > 10% | Stop line, root-cause with Master | Master, QC |
| Capacity overload | Rebalance across hubs / vendors | Leadership |
| Retailer approval pending > 7 days | Chaser email + call | Retail partner, Creative Director |

## 5. Reporting Pack (used by the app)

1. **Daily WIP report** — every style, current stage, days in stage.
2. **Weekly delivery-risk report** — orders at risk vs. promised date.
3. **Capacity report** — load per hub / cutter / stitcher.
4. **Costing reconciliation** — order-level cost vs. budget, with accounts.
5. **Dispatch & handover log** — packing, QC sign-off, dispatch date.

## 6. Role Boundaries (Protect the Master's Domain)

- Manager controls **workflow, timeline, data, coordination**.
- Master controls **pattern, fit, construction** — never overridden on technique.
- All fit/quality decisions flow through the Master; the Manager enforces deadlines and approvals.
